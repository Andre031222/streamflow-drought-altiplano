**Title:** Honest Baselines for Streamflow and Hydrological-Drought Forecasting in the Tropical Andes: An Open, Leakage-Free Multi-Station Benchmark with an Altiplano Focus

**Authors:** Richar Andre Vilca-Solorzano, Dina Maribel Yana-Yucra, Renato Quispe-Vargas. Faculty of Statistical and Computer Engineering, Universidad Nacional del Altiplano, Puno, Peru

Short-range streamflow forecasts and early drought signals matter in the tropical Andes, yet reported machine-learning gains are hard to compare because evaluation is rarely free of information leakage. We release an open benchmark of 19 long-record gauges from the Andean Glacierized Catchment dataset: 7 on the Altiplano, including Puente Isla Cabanillas in the Lake Titicaca basin, 6 in the Cordillera Blanca and 6 in the western and southern Andes. The gauges are combined with ERA5 and MERRA-2 reanalysis forcing and GloFAS v4 simulated discharge. Persistence and climatology are compared with five families of learners at 1-, 3- and 7-day leads, under a calendar-continuous, embargoed and validation-tuned protocol with block-bootstrap confidence intervals, Diebold–Mariano tests and walk-forward refits. The learners beat persistence at 15 of 19 gauges at one day and at 18 of 19 at seven days, with a median skill of 0.101. At Cabanillas, the one-day skill is 0.110 (95% confidence interval 0.064–0.144), which reverses an earlier result obtained with a leaky pipeline. A lower root-mean-square error did not improve the detection of drought days: the median critical success index is 0.43 for persistence and 0.30 for the learners. Hydrological drought decorrelates over about 582 km, and forecast skill follows the hydroclimatic regime rather than location.

**Keywords:** Streamflow forecasting · Hydrological drought · Altiplano · Machine learning · Persistence baseline · Data leakage · Spatial statistics · Open benchmark
