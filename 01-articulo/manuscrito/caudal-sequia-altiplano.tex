\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Communities and irrigated agriculture in the Peruvian Altiplano depend on highly seasonal streamflow into the Lake Titicaca system. Climate variability, glacier retreat in headwaters and limited real-time tooling make even short-range discharge forecasts valuable for water allocation and drought awareness. Large-sample machine-learning (ML) hydrology has shown that data-driven models can compete with process models when trained carefully \cite{kratzert2019lstm,gauch2021lstm}, and open catchment datasets such as CAMELS have set a reproducibility standard for multi-basin benchmarking \cite{addor2017camels}. Yet many published Andean pipelines remain hard to reuse because gauges, licences and code are fragmented.

This paper delivers a laptop-scale, open-data multi-station Peru benchmark with Altiplano focus. Concretely, we:
\begin{enumerate}
\item assemble a documented provenance stack---Andean-GC discharge \cite{aguayo2026andeangc} plus Open-Meteo/ERA5-Land precipitation \cite{munozsabater2021era5land,hersbach2020era5}---for \nStations{} Peru gauges with $\ge$5000 quality-controlled (QC) days;
\item enforce leakage-free chronological validation and an expanding-window walk-forward check on the Altiplano focus gauge;
\item report NSE \cite{nash1970}, KGE \cite{klinggupta2009,knoben2019kge} and skill versus persistence \cite{murphy1988skill}, so short-lead autocorrelation is not mistaken for model skill;
\item publish a parametric SSI-30 drought indicator (month-wise $z$-score of the 30-day mean) as a transparent but simplified companion to distributional SSI/SRI practice \cite{vicenteserrano2012ssi,shukla2008sri}, with open code and figures at \texttt{\githubUrl}.
\end{enumerate}
\noindent\textbf{What we do not claim.} We do not replace SENAMHI operational forecasts; we do not assert superiority of deep learning over linear models at these leads; and SSI-30 here is not a full Vicente-Serrano distributional SSI product.

This hydrology paper continues an open Altiplano research line by the same team (frost/$T_{\min}$ multi-station climate ML \cite{torres2025frost}; AgroYachay cloud IoT/LLM for Andean smallholders \cite{torres2026agroyachay}; AgroCommish desktop ESP32 commissioning on Zenodo \cite{vilca2026agrocommish}, SoftwareX in preparation---not cited as a published SoftwareX article). The present work is the open streamflow/drought-analysis branch of that lineage, not a farm-IoT systems paper.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Data-driven streamflow models are now common in mountain basins. Random forests have been used for short-lead runoff and flash-flood forecasting in tropical Andean catchments \cite{munoz2018rf,munoz2025rf}; support-vector regression has been tested for monthly runoff in the Central Andes of Argentina \cite{teverovsky2023svr}. In Peru, neural networks have been applied to monthly forecasts in high-Andean basins such as Crisnejas \cite{ramos2023crisnejas}, and $k$-nearest neighbours to daily Ramis (Altiplano) flows \cite{quispe2023ramis}. Large-sample LSTM hydrology and multi-timescale rainfall--runoff networks provide methodological context for honest ML baselines \cite{kratzert2019lstm,gauch2021lstm}. Our prior Altiplano frost/$T_{\min}$ multi-station climate-ML study \cite{torres2025frost} shares the comparative-benchmark ethos. Complementary work by Torres-Cruz and coauthors addresses climate vulnerability of agricultural yields in southern Peru with Bayesian spatial (CAR) models \cite{torres2026bayescar} and blocked/hierarchical cross-validation under spatial autocorrelation for Peruvian agricultural surveys \cite{torres2026blockedcv}---methodological cautions we follow by preferring chronological and walk-forward splits over shuffled folds. AgroYachay and AgroCommish \cite{torres2026agroyachay,vilca2026agrocommish} are related Andean open-software lineage (farm IoT), not hydrology papers.

Hydrological drought monitoring often relies on standardized indices. The Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) tradition and its runoff/streamflow analogues (SRI/SSI) motivate transparent, scale-aware drought indication \cite{shukla2008sri,vicenteserrano2012ssi}; the SPEI further incorporates atmospheric demand \cite{vicenteserrano2010spei}. Our SSI-30 is a simplified month-wise $z$-score of rolling discharge for the open benchmark---not a full distributional SSI. Reanalysis precipitation comes via Open-Meteo from ERA5-Land \cite{munozsabater2021era5land,hersbach2020era5}. Skill reporting follows NSE, KGE, watershed-model guidelines and classical mean-square skill scores \cite{nash1970,klinggupta2009,knoben2019kge,moriasi2007,murphy1988skill}.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Features}
For lead times $h\in\{1,7\}$ days we predict $Q_{t+h}$ from \nFeatures{} predictors: Fourier seasonality ($\sin$/$\cos$ of day-of-year), discharge lags $\{1,2,3,7,14,30\}$, rolling means and standard deviations over $\{7,14,30\}$ days, and precipitation lags/rolls $\{0,1,2,3,7\}$ and $\{7,30\}$.

\subsection{Models and baselines}
Baselines: persistence ($Q_{t+h}=Q_t$) and seasonal climatology (mean discharge by day-of-year on the training set). Learners: Ridge regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost and a small multilayer perceptron. All fits run on a commodity laptop CPU (full multi-station run $\approx$\runtimeSec{}\,s).

\subsection{Evaluation}
We use a chronological 70/15/15 split (train/validation/test) with no shuffling on every station. Test metrics are RMSE, MAE, NSE, KGE \cite{klinggupta2009} and skill versus persistence,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{skill}=1-\frac{\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{model}}}{\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{persistence}}}.
\end{equation}
Positive skill means improvement over persistence. On the focus gauge we additionally run an expanding-window walk-forward protocol (three folds) for Ridge versus persistence. We also compute SSI-30 as the month-wise $z$-score of the 30-day rolling mean discharge (parametric approximation; Fig.~\ref{fig:ssi}). This is not the full Vicente-Serrano distributional transform and should not be treated as an operational SSI product \cite{vicenteserrano2012ssi}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Four results structure the narrative (all numbers from the chronological test / walk-forward runs summarised in \texttt{cifras.tex}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$h=1$: persistence wins.} At Cabanillas, persistence NSE~$=$\nsePersHOne{} (KGE~$=$\kgePersHOne{}) exceeds every learner; best ML is MLP (NSE~$=$\nseMlpHOne{}) but remains skill-negative on RMSE.
\item \textbf{$h=7$: Ridge gains modest skill.} Ridge NSE~$=$\nseRidgeHSeven{} vs.\ persistence \nsePersHSeven{} (skill~$=$\skillRidgeHSeven{}; KGE~$=$\kgeRidgeHSeven{}); MLP is close behind.
\item \textbf{Multi-station:} best ML skill is positive at all \nPosSkillHSeven{}/\nStations{} gauges (median skill~$=$\medianBestSkillHSeven{}); Ridge alone is skill-positive everywhere (mean skill~$=$\meanSkillRidgeHSeven{}).
\item \textbf{Walk-forward:} expanding-window Ridge skill at $h=7$ is positive in all three Cabanillas folds (mean skill~$=$\wfMeanSkillHSeven{}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The one-day result is intentional honesty: strong autocorrelation makes ``ML superiority'' without a persistence baseline misleading \cite{murphy1988skill,knoben2019kge}. The seven-day Ridge/MLP gain is modest yet reproducible across all \nStations{} gauges, obtained with open data and about \runtimeSec{}~s of laptop CPU. Walk-forward folds support the chronological $h=7$ skill signal.

Limitations: (i)~a single reanalysis precipitation source (Open-Meteo/ERA5-Land), not local SENAMHI rain gauges; (ii)~no routing or process constraints; (iii)~parametric SSI-30 rather than a full distributional SSI \cite{vicenteserrano2012ssi,shukla2008sri}; (iv)~heterogeneous record eras (Cordillera Blanca historical vs.\ modern Altiplano); (v)~no formal uncertainty quantification or operational assimilation. Natural extensions include more Altiplano gauges when QC length allows, distributional SSI/SPEI products \cite{vicenteserrano2010spei}, and LSTM/transfer learning following large-sample practice \cite{kratzert2019lstm,gauch2021lstm}. This work does not replace SENAMHI or institutional warning systems.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{Main takeaway.} An open, laptop-scale Peru multi-station benchmark with Altiplano focus shows that persistence remains the strongest one-day baseline (Cabanillas NSE~$=$\nsePersHOne{}), while lightweight Ridge models deliver modest but consistent seven-day skill over persistence on all \nStations{} long gauges (focus skill~$=$\skillRidgeHSeven{}; multi-station median best-ML skill~$=$\medianBestSkillHSeven{}), with walk-forward confirmation (mean skill~$=$\wfMeanSkillHSeven{}).

\textbf{Limitations.} Results rest on a single precip source, a parametric SSI-30 (not operational distributional SSI), and research-grade chronological splits---not a SENAMHI replacement.

\textbf{Future work.} Expand the Altiplano gauge set when QC length allows; adopt distributional SSI/SPEI; and test LSTM/transfer learning under the same persistence-honest protocol. Full code and metrics: \texttt{\githubUrl}.
